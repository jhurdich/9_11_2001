September 11, 2001 public record

The attacks and the national response

Four commercial aircraft were hijacked. Two struck the World Trade Center, one struck the Pentagon, and one crashed near Shanksville, Pennsylvania. The federal 9/11 Commission later examined the plot, preparedness, and immediate response.

September 20, 2001 public record

The President addresses the nation

President George W. Bush addressed Congress and the country, framing the attacks as an act of terrorism and outlining the government’s immediate course of action.

November 27, 2002 → July 22, 2004 public record

Investigation becomes a lasting document

Congress and the President established the National Commission on Terrorist Attacks Upon the United States. Its final report was transmitted in 2004 and became a central public document for understanding 9/11.

Greater Boston context

What was documented in the area before 9/11?

Yes—people connected to al-Qaeda or other terrorism investigations were documented in the wider Boston area before September 11, 2001. The evidence should be described carefully: some were identified as associates or operatives, while the public record does not establish that every person below participated in the 9/11 plot.

Dorchester

Nabil al-Marabh lived in Dorchester from approximately 1989 until September 2000. A contemporaneous report described him as an alleged al-Qaeda operative and reported links involving hijackers Satam al-Suqami and Ahmed al-Ghamdi.

Everett

Raed Hijazi, who had connections to al-Qaeda and was later convicted in Jordan in a terrorism-related plot, lived in Everett and worked as a Boston cab driver before leaving the region in 1999.

Brighton

Contemporary reporting said investigators were examining a Days Inn in Brighton where some hijackers were suspected of staying. This is reported lodging information, not proof that the men lived there long-term.

Logan Airport

On September 11, 10 hijackers passed through Boston Logan: five boarded American Airlines Flight 11 and five boarded United Airlines Flight 175. The 9/11 Commission documents those airport movements directly.

What this establishes: The broader Boston metro area had documented al-Qaeda-linked people and activity before 9/11, placing your East Boston residence within the wider regional context of the period.
Sources: Boston Herald, September 19, 2001 · 9/11 Commission, “We Have Some Planes”.
